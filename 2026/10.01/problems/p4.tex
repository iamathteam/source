Martin's rescue party must travel from $(0,0)$ to $(6,6)$ on the road
network below. Every move is one unit north or one unit east. Enemy
checkpoints at $(2,2)$ and $(4,4)$ cannot be visited.
How many shortest routes remain?

\begin{center}
\begin{asy}
unitsize(0.69cm);
for (int i=0; i<=6; ++i) {
  draw((i,0)--(i,6),black+0.55bp);
  draw((0,i)--(6,i),black+0.55bp);
}
pair S=(0,0), T=(6,6), X=(2,2), Y=(4,4);
dot(S,black+4bp); dot(T,black+4bp);
label("$S=(0,0)$",S,SW,black); label("$T=(6,6)$",T,NE,black);
for (pair P : new pair[]{X,Y}) {
  draw(P+(-0.13,-0.13)--P+(0.13,0.13),black+1.5bp);
  draw(P+(-0.13,0.13)--P+(0.13,-0.13),black+1.5bp);
}
label("$(2,2)$",X,SW,black);
label("$(4,4)$",Y,NE,black);
\end{asy}
\end{center}

\begin{solution}
Every shortest route consists of six east and six north moves, so there
are $\binom{12}{6}=924$ without restrictions.
There are $\binom42\binom84=420$ routes through $(2,2)$ and,
symmetrically, $420$ through $(4,4)$. Routes through both checkpoints
were subtracted twice: their number is $\binom42^3=216$.
By inclusion--exclusion, the answer is
\[
\boxed{924-420-420+216=300}.
\]
\end{solution}
