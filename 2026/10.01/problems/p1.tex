Supreme Commander Varun Gadi has concealed the size of the army guarding
Prince Martin Qi Ho. His quartermaster offers a smaller example: a detachment
of fewer than 50 soldiers leaves 2 soldiers over when arranged in rows of 3,
3 over in rows of 5, and 2 over in rows of 7. How many soldiers are in the
detachment?

The real army is much larger. Its head count is between 60,000 and 70,000.
Arranging the soldiers in rows of 7, 9, 11, and 13 leaves, respectively,
3, 2, 1, and 3 soldiers over. Determine its exact size.

\begin{solution}
For the small detachment, the conditions are
\[
 n\equiv 2\pmod 3,\qquad n\equiv 3\pmod 5,\qquad
 n\equiv 2\pmod 7.
\]
The first and third give $n\equiv 2\pmod{21}$. Of $2,23,44$,
only $23$ leaves remainder $3$ modulo $5$. Thus $\boxed{n=23}$.

For the army, the first and fourth reports give $N\equiv3\pmod{91}$,
so write $N=3+91k$. The remaining reports imply
\[
 3+k\equiv2\pmod9,\qquad 3+3k\equiv1\pmod{11},
\]
hence $k\equiv8\pmod9$ and $k\equiv3\pmod{11}$.
Writing $k=8+9t$ gives $9t\equiv6\pmod{11}$, so
$t\equiv8\pmod{11}$. Therefore
\[
 N=3+91\bigl(8+9(8+11u)\bigr)=7283+9009u.
\]
The only value between 60,000 and 70,000 occurs at $u=6$:
$\boxed{N=61{,}337}$.
\end{solution}
