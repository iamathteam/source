Five messengers intercepted values from Martin's secret quadratic code
$f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$. Varun replaced exactly one value with a forgery.
Identify the forged report and recover the secret $f(0)$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{Messenger }x&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
\text{Reported }f(x)&27&35&53&80&119
\end{array}
\]

\begin{solution}
A quadratic sampled at consecutive integers has constant second differences.
The first three reports have first differences $8,18$, hence second
difference $10$. Continuing that pattern predicts first differences
$8,18,28,38$ and values $27,35,53,81,119$.
Thus messenger $4$ supplied the forgery: $\boxed{80\text{ should be }81}$.
Continuing the first differences backward gives $-2$ between $f(0)$ and
$f(1)$; therefore $\boxed{f(0)=29}$.
Equivalently, the recovered polynomial is $f(x)=5x^2-7x+29$.
The answer is unique: two distinct quadratics cannot each agree with
at least four of five reports, since they would share at least three points.
\end{solution}
