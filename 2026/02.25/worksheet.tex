\problem A square with each side of length $1$ is inscribed in a circle. What is the length of a side of a square which is inscribed in one of the circular zones outside the given square? This square has two vertices on the side of the square with sides of length $1$ and the other two vertices on the circle.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  x=2.1cm, y=2.1cm,
  line cap=round, line join=round,
  every node/.style={font=\small}
]

% Big square: side 1, centered at origin
\pgfmathsetmacro{\a}{0.5}            % half side length
\pgfmathsetmacro{\r}{sqrt(2)/2}      % circumradius of the big square

% Small square side s determined by: (s/2)^2 + (a+s)^2 = r^2  -> 5s^2+4s-1=0 -> s=1/5
\pgfmathsetmacro{\s}{(-4 + sqrt(36))/10} % = 0.2

\coordinate (O) at (0,0);
\coordinate (A) at (-\a,-\a);
\coordinate (B) at ( \a,-\a);
\coordinate (C) at ( \a, \a);
\coordinate (D) at (-\a, \a);

% Small square sits on top side CD, centered horizontally
\coordinate (E) at (-\s/2,\a);
\coordinate (F) at ( \s/2,\a);
\coordinate (G) at ( \s/2,\a+\s);
\coordinate (H) at (-\s/2,\a+\s);

% Circle (slightly lighter so it doesn't visually "double up" with square edges)
\draw[thick] (O) circle (\r);

% Big square
\draw[thick] (A)--(B)--(C)--(D)--cycle;

% Small square
\draw[thick] (E)--(F)--(G)--(H)--cycle;

% Mark the "on-circle" vertices (optional but nice)
\fill (G) circle (0.45pt);
\fill (H) circle (0.45pt);

% Minimal label for side length 1 (no arrows)
\node[below] at ($(A)!0.5!(B)$) {$1$};

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\problem Suppose $x$ and $y$ are real numbers such that $x-\dfrac{1}{x}=y+\dfrac{1}{y}$. What is the value of
$x^2+\frac{1}{x^2}-\left(y^2+\frac{1}{y^2}\right)$?
\choicesH{$-4$}{$0$}{ $2$ }{ $4$ }{Cannot be determined.}

\problem You play on a game show with the following rules. You are shown four prizes with different prices. You are given four price tags which give the prices of the prizes, but you don't know which tag goes with which prize. You are asked to match the price tags to the prizes, and for each correct match, you win the prize. Unfortunately, you have no idea how much any of the prizes cost, and so you place the price tags randomly. What is the most likely number of prizes you will win?
\choicesH{$0$}{$1$}{$2$}{$3$}{$4$}

\problem Five different colored pieces of candy are to be given to three children. How many ways can this be done so that each child gets at least one piece of candy?
\choicesH{$125$}{$150$}{$175$}{$225$}{$250$}

\problem Let $f(x)$ be a function which is defined for all real numbers $x$. Suppose that for every real number $x$, we have
\[
f(x+3)f(x-3)=9.
\]
Also, for $-3<x\le 3$, we have $f(x)=\abs{x}$. What is the value of $f(67)$?
\choicesH{$-1$}{$1$}{$3$}{$9$}{$67$}

\problem The polynomial $f(x)=4x^3-2x^2+7x-9$ has two complex conjugate roots. What is the modulus (or absolute value) of such a complex root?
\choicesH{$\dfrac{1}{2}$}{$1$}{$\dfrac{3}{2}$}{$\dfrac{9}{4}$}{$4$}

\problem If $f(x)=\dfrac{4^x}{4^x+2}$, then determine the value of
\[
f(0)+f\!\paren{\frac{1}{2026}}+f\!\paren{\frac{2}{2026}}+f\!\paren{\frac{3}{2026}}+\cdots
+f\!\paren{\frac{2024}{2026}}+f\!\paren{\frac{2025}{2026}}+f(1).
\]
\choicesH{$1003$}{$\dfrac{2025}{2}$}{$1013$}{$\dfrac{2027}{2}$}{$2026$}

\problem Carlos took $70\%$ of a whole pie. Maria took one third of the remainder. What portion of the whole pie was left?
\choicesH{$10\%$}{$15\%$}{$20\%$}{$30\%$}{$35\%$}

\problem The $25$ integers from $-10$ to $14$ inclusive can be arranged to form a $5$-by-$5$ square in which the sum of the numbers in each row, the sum of the numbers in each column, and the sum of the numbers along each of the main diagonals are all the same. What is the value of this common sum?
\choicesH{$2$}{$5$}{$10$}{$25$}{$50$}

\problem How many ordered pairs of integers $(x,y)$ satisfy the equation $x^{2026}+y^2=2y$?
\choicesH{$1$}{$2$}{$3$}{$4$}{infinitely many solutions}


\problem For all integers $n\ge 9$, the value of
\[
\frac{(n+2)!-(n+1)!}{n!}
\]
is always which of the following?
\choicesH{divisible by $4$}{divisible by $10$}{a perfect square}{a perfect cube}{a prime}


\problem Bela and Jenn play the following game on the closed interval $[0,n]$ of the real number line, where $n$ is a fixed integer greater than $4$. They take turns playing, with Bela going first. At his first turn, Bela chooses any real number in the interval $[0,n]$. Thereafter, the player whose turn it is chooses a real number that is more than one unit away from all numbers previously chosen by either player. A player unable to choose such a number loses. Using optimal strategy, which player will win the game?
\choices{Bela always wins}{Jenn always wins}{Bela will win if and only if $n$ is odd}{Jenn will win if and only if $n$ is odd}{Bela will win if and only if $n>8$}

\problem What is the sum of all real numbers $x$ for which the median of the numbers $4,6,8,17,$ and $x$ is equal to the mean of those five numbers?
\choicesH{$-5$}{$0$}{$5$}{$\dfrac{15}{4}$}{$\dfrac{35}{4}$}
