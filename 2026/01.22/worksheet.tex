\section{Basic Practice (no Euclidean Algorithm)}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2.85cm]
    \item Sreyas has 1 pile of spaghetti and one pile of sushi. He has 36 slices of spaghetti and 60 sushi rolls. Sreyas wants to split the two piles into lunch for his birthday. If each kid must get the same number of spaghetti as every other kid and  each kid must get the same number of sushi as every other kid, what is the maximum number of people he could invite to lunch on his birthday while satisfiying all of the conditions?
    \item Rohan rudely robs 3 people for money. One person gives 20 dollars, another gives 32 dollars and a third gives 35 dollars. What is the least common multiple of these three numbers?
    \item \textit{Rohan's Rockets} ricochet really rapidly at the moon. Rocket Type Zeus is ricocheted every 30 minutes, Rocket Type Hera is ricocheted every 45 minutes and Rocket Type Hermes is ricocheted every 75 minutes. If each was ricocheted at noon, when will be the next time they are all ricocheted together?
    \item Marty is racing around the death star against Luke Skywalker. Marty is a great pilot so he is able to circle the planet every 90 minutes. Luke's starfighter circled every 80 minutes. If they were traveling in the same direction, how long will it take them to meet in the same spot they started their race in?
    \item Darth Varun has a need for his suit to be updated every 12 days. He also needs to charge his suit every 30 days. If he updated and charged on the twenty-second of January, when will be the next time he performs both tasks on the same day?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Euclidean Algorithm Practice}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Find the LCM and GCD of the following pairs of numbers\: 
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item 756 and 132
    \item 1024 and 792
    \item 1560 and 1170
    \item 548 and 286
    \item 2310 and 462
    \end{enumerate}
    
    \item Two planets, Nahorland and Lagnabland, orbit their star in fixed durations measured in Earth days. Nahorland completes one orbit every 24,732 days, while Lagnabland completes one orbit every 36,945 days. You are an astronomer tasked with predicting when both planets will next align perfectly relative to their star.

    \item \textit{Sneak Peek for Next Practice}: Lockheed Martin is remaking a many of their Blackbird planes to replace modern day cars. They have $607^2$ planes which they need to distribute to $41$ companies. They will donate all leftover planes to IA Math Team. How many planes are left over which they will donate to us?
\end{enumerate}
